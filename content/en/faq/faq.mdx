Questions about the vaporetto cluster into the same handful across search boxes, travel forums and first-time visitors' conversations. This page answers them straight — definitions precise, prices indicative, and pointers into this site's deeper guides for the long answers.

## What Does Vaporetto Mean?

A vaporetto is a motorboat serving as a canal bus in Venice, Italy. The word comes from Italian vapore (steam) combined with the diminutive suffix -etto, literally meaning little steamer — a name earned by the steam-powered boats that first served Venice's canals in the 19th century. The plural is vaporetti. The complete guide covers the vaporetto meaning in full detail.

## How Much Does a Vaporetto Ticket Cost?

A single vaporetto ticket costs approximately €9.50 (recent pricing; verify current fares), valid for 75 minutes. Travel passes — 24-hour, 48-hour, 72-hour and 7-day — offer unlimited rides at progressively better value. The tickets and prices guide covers current fares and the pass-versus-single calculation.

## What Is the Plural of Vaporetto?

The plural of vaporetto is vaporetti, following the standard Italian masculine noun pattern (-o becomes -i). English texts occasionally use vaporettos, but vaporetti is the preferred and more common plural.

## Is the Vaporetto the Same as a Gondola?

No — they are entirely different. The vaporetto is Venice's public waterbus: scheduled, motorized, carrying dozens of passengers at bus-fare prices. The gondola is a traditional rowing boat for private hire, costing €80-100+ for a 30-minute ride. The comparison page covers all the differences in detail.

## Which Vaporetto Line Should I Take?

For the Grand Canal scenic route: Line 1 (every stop, the classic). For faster crossings: Line 2 (limited stops). For Murano: Lines 4.1, 4.2 or the direct services. For Burano: Line 12 from Fondamente Nove. The routes and lines page maps the full network.

## Do I Need to Validate My Vaporetto Ticket?

Yes — every time, before every boarding. Hold your ticket or pass against the electronic reader on the platform until you hear the beep and see the green light. Riding without validation incurs significant fines. This is the single most important vaporetto rule.

## Can I Take Luggage on the Vaporetto?

Yes — each ticket includes one piece of luggage. Manage it as you would on a city bus: keep it close, don't block aisles. For large cruise-style suitcases during peak hours, consider a water taxi for the initial hotel transfer.

## Does the Vaporetto Run at Night?

Yes — reduced night service (the N lines) operates on key routes including the Grand Canal corridor, roughly every 30-60 minutes. The night vaporetto is also one of Venice's quiet pleasures: the canal under lights without the daytime crowds.

## Where Do I Buy Vaporetto Tickets?

At ACTV ticket booths at major stops (Ferrovia, Piazzale Roma, Rialto, San Marco), automated machines at most landings, the AVM Venezia mobile app, and authorized retailers including many hotels. The practical advice: buy before your first ride, not at the stop where the boat is already arriving.

## Is the Vaporetto Worth It?

For most visitors, yes — unambiguously. The vaporetto is the most practical, most affordable and most scenic way to navigate Venice. Line 1 along the Grand Canal doubles as one of the world's great boat tours at bus-fare prices, and the network reaches every destination a visitor needs. Buy a pass, validate diligently, ride the Line 1 at golden hour — and the vaporetto meaning becomes clear: Venice's bus, Venice's best tour, and Venice itself.
