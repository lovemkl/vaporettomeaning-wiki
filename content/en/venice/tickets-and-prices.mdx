The vaporetto fare structure is transit-style and transparent: pay per journey or buy a pass. This page covers the current ticket types, prices, validity rules, purchase options and the discounts most visitors miss. Understanding the vaporetto meaning is free; riding one has a price — and the right ticket choice saves real money.

## Single Tickets

The single-journey vaporetto ticket costs around €9.50 (as of recent ACTV updates; always verify current pricing at a ticket booth or the official ACTV website). The single ticket is valid for 75 minutes from first validation and permits travel on any vaporetto line in one direction — you can change lines within the 75-minute window but cannot reverse direction. The ticket includes one piece of luggage. The single ticket makes sense for one-off crossings: a single Grand Canal traversal, a one-way hop to Murano. For anything more than two rides in a day, a pass is the better answer.

## Travel Passes: 24 / 48 / 72 Hours and 7 Days

The ACTV travel passes offer unlimited vaporetto rides for a flat price: 24-hour, 48-hour, 72-hour and 7-day cards, each valid from first validation (not from purchase). The pass economics are straightforward: the 24-hour pass costs roughly the price of two single tickets, and any day with three or more vaporetto journeys — which describes virtually every visitor's day in Venice — makes the pass the cheaper option. The multi-day passes reduce the per-day cost further, and the 7-day card is the best value for week-long stays.

The pass includes luggage allowance (one item per journey) and covers all ACTV vaporetto lines and the mainland bus services. The passes do not cover the Alilaguna airport boats (a separate operator) or the gondolas (a private service). Passes are non-transferable and require the same validation as single tickets — hold against the reader at each boarding.

## Rolling Venice Card

The Rolling Venice Card is the discount card for young travellers aged 14 to 29, purchased for a small fee and valid for one year. It includes a discounted vaporetto pass option (a 72-hour unlimited card at a fraction of the standard price), plus discounts at museums, attractions and businesses throughout Venice. For backpackers, students and young couples, the Rolling Venice Card is one of Italy's best travel deals — the card's vaporetto component alone can save €30+ over standard pass pricing. Purchase at ACTV ticket booths with proof of age.

## Where to Buy

Tickets and passes are available at four channels: ACTV ticket booths at major vaporetto stops (Ferrovia/Santa Lucia, Piazzale Roma, Rialto, San Marco Vallaresso, and other large landings); automated ticket machines at most stops (touch-screen, multilingual, accept cards and cash); the AVM Venezia official app (purchase, store and validate tickets on your phone — also shows real-time vaporetto departures); and authorized retailers including hotel front desks, newsstands and tabacchi shops. The practical advice: buy your pass before your first ride — not at the stop where the vaporetto is already approaching.

## Validation: The Step You Cannot Skip

Every vaporetto ticket and pass must be validated at the electronic readers on the boarding platform before each journey. Hold your ticket or card against the reader until you hear the beep and see the green light. Validation is enforced by inspectors who board vaporetti periodically and check all passengers' tickets; riding without a validated ticket incurs a significant fine (over €50 on the spot, higher if not paid immediately), and "I forgot to validate" is not accepted as an excuse. The validation requirement applies to every boarding — including pass-holders on their tenth ride of the day. The habit to form: approach the reader, validate, then board. The habit to break: stepping onto the vaporetto first and thinking about the ticket later.
